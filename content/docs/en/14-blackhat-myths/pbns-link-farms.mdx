## Concept

A **PBN** (private blog network) is a controlled or rented group of sites used primarily to create ranking links to target sites. A **link farm** is a site or network that produces large volumes of low-value cross-links for the same purpose. These patterns fall under link spam; repurposing expired domains mainly for old authority can also be expired-domain abuse.

A site is not a PBN merely because it is private, shares ownership, uses WordPress Multisite, sits on one CDN, or belongs to a publisher portfolio. The policy line is **link-manipulation purpose**. Real brands may share navigation or cite each other when that helps users. A network becomes abusive when properties or pages exist chiefly to manufacture ranking credit.

This topic covers recognition, governance, and cleanup. It does not provide instructions for hiding network ownership or evading detection.

## Why it matters

Network links are an unstable dependency: they may be ignored, removed, or associated with a manual action. Acquisitions can inherit the liability through old templates, expired-domain redirects, or vendor contracts.

<CorrectExample>
A company operates a product site, documentation site, and status site. Each has a real audience and distinct purpose. Global navigation clearly identifies the relationship; contextual links appear only when useful.

```tsx
<nav aria-label="Company services">
  <a href="https://example.com/">Product</a>
  <a href="https://docs.example.com/">Documentation</a>
  <a href="https://status.example.com/">Service status</a>
</nav>
```

The sites would still deserve to exist if search engines passed no ranking credit.
</CorrectExample>

<IncorrectExample why="The acquired domains and articles have no independent audience or purpose; their job is to send exact-match links to a target URL. That is link manipulation.">
A vendor buys unrelated expired domains, posts thin “reviews,” and inserts the same commercial anchor to one client. Sitewide footer links connect the nodes. The contract promises a number of ranking links, not readership, referral traffic, or editorial review.
</IncorrectExample>

## WordPress implementation

**Yoast SEO**

1. Correct canonicals and sitemaps do not legitimize a network designed for links.
2. Audit theme footers, reusable blocks, author bios, and imported posts for inherited commercial links.

**Rank Math**

1. Do not use Redirections to funnel unrelated expired domains into money pages.
2. Export redirect and link data during cleanup so removals are reviewable.

**AIOSEO**

1. Multisite SEO settings are appropriate for genuine properties, not evidence that a link network is compliant.
2. Mark paid placements with `rel="sponsored"`; do not promise ranking credit.

**Feature gap:** No plugin can infer the commercial purpose or ownership graph behind a link. Contracts, invoices, editorial records, host/domain history, and backlink review are required.

## Next.js implementation

Keep links in ordinary, reviewable components and add the appropriate relationship for paid placements:

```tsx
type PartnerLinkProps = {
  href: string;
  label: string;
  paid: boolean;
};

export function PartnerLink({ href, label, paid }: PartnerLinkProps) {
  return (
    <a href={href} rel={paid ? "sponsored" : undefined}>
      {label}
    </a>
  );
}
```

In the App Router, do not generate a sitewide outbound-link graph from a domain list, and do not use `proxy.ts` to show Googlebot different links. Remove abusive links at their source; use `410` for retired network content when no relevant replacement exists.

Pages Router note: the same applies to `_app`, `getStaticProps`, and API-fed footer components. Framework choice does not turn controlled cross-linking into editorial endorsement.

## Common mistakes and outdated myths

- **DEPRECATED:** “A unique theme and separate IP make a PBN safe.”
- **DEPRECATED:** “A private site or private network is automatically a PBN.”
- **DEPRECATED:** “Disavow is a routine insurance policy after every paid-link campaign.”
- Treating three-way exchanges as a policy loophole.
- Redirecting every inherited expired domain to the strongest commercial page.
- Calling a link farm “digital PR” without a real publisher, audience, or editorial decision.

## Senior / production notes

Vendor due diligence should ask what is being purchased. Pay for legitimate creative, research, outreach, or sponsorship—not guaranteed ranking links. Keep live placement URLs, relationship attributes, invoices, and editorial contacts.

For an inherited scheme: stop new placements, inventory links and redirects, remove what you control, request third-party removal where practical, and consider disavow for unresolved artificial links. If a manual action exists, submit a documented reconsideration request after substantive cleanup.

Do not 301 retired network nodes into the target site; that can concentrate the same problem. Do not publish “footprint avoidance” guidance. The durable test is whether each property and link serves people without ranking credit.

## How to verify

| Check | Pass | Fail |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Property purpose | Real audience and independent utility | Exists chiefly to link |
| Placement record | Editorial or disclosed sponsorship | Guaranteed ranking link |
| Anchor/template crawl | Natural, contextual references | Repeated exact-match footers |
| Domain history | Relevant continuity | Unrelated expired-domain repurpose |
| GSC/manual actions | Clean | Unnatural links action |
